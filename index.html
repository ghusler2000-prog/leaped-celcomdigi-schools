The national picture

Four in ten Malaysian children finish primary school below the minimum reading level.

If the national rate held in the 15 schools on this map, about 5,770 of their 13,740 pupils would reach the end of primary school below the minimum reading level.

From our own work: in the 249 Selangor primary schools in the government's Programme Anak Kita, chosen as among the lowest performing in their districts, we assessed the pupils identified for remedial support. 97 in every 100 of them scored in the red band, 0 to 4 out of 10, on at least one basic reading or maths skill. Over the 2025 school year, 86 in 100 of them moved out of the red band on at least one of those skills.

By 15 the gap shows in every subject. In PISA 2025 Malaysia scored below the OECD average in reading, maths and science. Its maths score fell 11 points on 2022, while reading rose 5 points and science 3.

How these schools were chosen

  • Government primary schools with at least 300 pupils.
  • Outside the Petaling Utama education district, where CelcomDigi Tower sits.
  • In parliamentary constituencies with a 2024 median household income below RM10,500 a month. Fifteen of the 34 constituencies in Selangor, Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya fall below that line. The 15 schools sit in Bandar Tun Razak (RM9,360), Cheras (RM10,228), Lembah Pantai (RM10,331).
  • Not already part of READy+. The 15 shown are the nearest to the tower. 62 schools within 16 km meet the same test.
  • The grey pins are schools near the tower that ran READy+ for the 2025 school year under the government's Programme Anak Kita. They are shown for context and are not on the list: SK Taman Dato Harun (2), SK Taman Dato' Harun (Satu), SK (2) Petaling Jaya, SK Satu Sultan Alam Shah, SJK(C) Yuk Chyun.

About the estimates. These are illustrations, not measurements. 'Expected below the minimum reading level' applies the World Bank's national rate of 42 per cent to each school's enrolment, rounded to the nearest 10. A baseline in each school replaces it with the real number.

Programme Anak Kita figures. 10,299 of the 10,662 pupils assessed at the start and the end were in the red band, scoring 0 to 4 out of 10, on at least one skill, and 8,817 of them were out of the red band on at least one skill by the end of the school year. Sources: 10,662 matched pupils, Programme READy+ Impact Report, LeapEd, April 2026, p.8. 10,299 and 8,817 as read from the READy+ Student Impact Dashboard on 8 October 2026. The schools were selected for the programme as among the lowest performing in their districts (LeapEd programme records).
